PROJECT TITLE: Larena — Accessible Audio Learning Platform for East African Youth

PROJECT DURATION: 12 months (May 2026 – December 2026)

TOTAL BUDGET REQUESTED: KSh 928,000 (approximately USD 6,960)

PROJECT LOCATION: Nairobi, Kenya (pilot in Kibera, Mathare, and Kiambu County)

PROJECT SUMMARY:

Larena is a digital platform that converts PDF documents — textbooks, study guides, 
and notes — into audio format, making education accessible to learners who prefer 
audio, struggle with reading, or lack reliable internet connectivity. The project 
combines open-source text-to-speech technology with community-based youth narrators 
to create curriculum-aligned audio content in Swahili, English, and local East 
African languages.

The project directly addresses three critical challenges:
1. Learning accessibility for students with reading difficulties and visual impairments
2. Youth unemployment by creating digital livelihood opportunities as audio content creators
3. Educational equity by delivering offline-capable learning tools to underserved communities

KEY HIGHLIGHTS:
• First 6 months: Build MVP Android app, pilot with 2 schools, validate with 500 users
• Months 7–12: Scale to 10 school partnerships, 2,000+ users, first revenue generation
• Employment creation: 10+ youth employed as narrators, community managers, and tech support
• Revenue model: Freemium public app + institutional licenses + publisher partnerships

EXPECTED OUTCOMES:
• 2,000+ students accessing curriculum content via audio
• 10+ unemployed youth trained and earning income as digital content creators
• 10 schools partnered with licensed access to audio curriculum
• Pathway to self-sustainability by Month 12

               SECTION 2: PROJECT BACKGROUND AND JUSTIFICATION

2.1 THE PROBLEM

In East Africa, millions of students face barriers to effective learning:

• READING DIFFICULTIES: An estimated 15–20% of Kenyan students struggle with 
  reading comprehension, yet curriculum delivery remains overwhelmingly text-based.

• LIMITED ACCESS TO TEXTBOOKS: The pupil-to-textbook ratio in Kenyan public 
  schools averages 3:1, meaning most students lack physical books to study at home.

• CONNECTIVITY GAPS: While smartphone penetration is growing, reliable internet 
  remains expensive and inconsistent, especially in informal settlements and rural areas.

• VISUAL IMPAIRMENTS: Students with visual disabilities are largely excluded from 
  mainstream curriculum access, with limited Braille materials and few audio alternatives.

• YOUTH UNEMPLOYMENT: Nairobi has over 1.5 million unemployed youth aged 18–35, 
  many with digital skills but no income-generating platform.

               SECTION 5: PROJECT DESCRIPTION AND METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

5.1 PROJECT COMPONENTS

COMPONENT 1: LARENA MOBILE APPLICATION

Description:
An Android application that allows users to:
• Upload PDF documents from device storage or cloud
• Extract text using optical character recognition (OCR) and native PDF parsing
• Convert text to audio using open-source text-to-speech (TTS) engines
• Download audio for offline listening
• Adjust playback speed, bookmark sections, and take voice notes
• Access pre-loaded curriculum-aligned audio content

Technical Architecture:
• Platform: Android (Kotlin/Java)
• Minimum SDK: API 21 (Android 5.0) — covers 95%+ of target devices
• PDF Parsing: Apache PDFBox or iText library
• OCR: Google ML Kit (on-device, no internet required)
• TTS Engine: Coqui TTS (open-source, supports Swahili and English)
• Audio Compression: OPUS codec for small file sizes
• Backend: Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Storage, Firestore Database)
• Offline Storage: Room Database + local file system
• Payment Integration: M-Pesa Daraja API

COMPONENT 2: COMMUNITY NARRATOR NETWORK

Description:
A distributed network of youth narrators who record high-quality audio content 
for curriculum materials, especially in languages where AI TTS is not yet mature.

Operations:
• Recruit narrators from BRACE4PEACE target communities
• Provide training: smartphone recording techniques, audio quality standards, 
  pronunciation guides
• Quality assurance: Peer review + automated audio quality checks
• Payment: Per-chapter basis (KSh 200–500 depending on length and complexity)
• Content pipeline: Narrators receive assignments via WhatsApp/Telegram, 
  submit via Google Drive or app upload

COMPONENT 3: CURRICULUM CONTENT LIBRARY

Description:
A growing repository of audio learning materials aligned with Kenyan curriculum.

Content Sources (Priority Order):
1. Open Educational Resources (OER) — zero copyright risk
2. Original study guides created by Larena team
3. Licensed publisher content (revenue-share agreements)
4. User-generated content (student notes, with moderation)

Subjects Covered (Phase 1):
• Mathematics (Form 1–4)
• Biology, Chemistry, Physics
• Kiswahili and English literature
• History and Government
• Geography

COMPONENT 4: TEACHER CHAMPION PROGRAM

Description:
Train teachers as Larena ambassadors who promote the app, troubleshoot issues, 
and collect feedback from students.

Structure:
• Select 2 teachers per pilot school
• 1-day training workshop on app usage and student onboarding
• Monthly virtual check-ins
• Stipend: KSh 2,000/month per teacher (for 6 months during pilot)
• Incentive: Free premium access, certificates, recognition at county level

                    SECTION 6: INNOVATION AND UNIQUENESS
================================================================================

6.1 WHAT MAKES LARENA DIFFERENT

GLOBAL COMPETITORS (Speechify, NaturalReader):
❌ Expensive ($139/year)
❌ English-centric, poor African language support
❌ Require fast, consistent internet
❌ No curriculum alignment
❌ No employment creation

AFRICAN AUDIO PROJECTS (AkooBooks, LEAP):
❌ Entertainment-focused or narrow scope
❌ Hardware-dependent (solar MP3 players)
❌ Limited language coverage
❌ Not interactive or personalized

LARENA'S UNIQUE VALUE PROPOSITION:
✅ Swahili and local language-first design
✅ Offline-first — works without internet
✅ Ultra-light — runs on $50 smartphones
✅ Curriculum-aligned with KICD standards
✅ Youth employment engine — community narrators
✅ Freemium model — free for students, institutional revenue
✅ Karaoke-style text highlighting for literacy bridging
✅ Voice note and quiz features for active learning

6.2 INNOVATION IN APPROACH

1. HYBRID AI + HUMAN MODEL
   Use AI TTS for well-resourced languages (Swahili, English) and human narrators 
   for under-resourced languages. This balances cost, quality, and employment creation.

2. FRUGAL INNOVATION DESIGN
   Every feature is tested on a KSh 8,000 smartphone with 500MB data. If it 
   doesn't work there, it doesn't ship.

3. COMMUNITY-OWNED CONTENT
   Narrators are not gig workers — they are stakeholders. Revenue-sharing 
   and cooperative decision-making build ownership and quality.

4. DATA-DRIVEN IMPACT
   Built-in analytics track not just downloads, but learning outcomes: 
   time spent per chapter, quiz scores, repeat listens, and teacher feedback.
